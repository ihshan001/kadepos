**About my app — everything you need to know**

**The one-line version:**
It's a billing and shop-management app for small shops in Sri Lanka. It runs on an ordinary Android phone and works completely without internet.

**What problem it solves:**
Most small shops here still use a paper bill book and a notebook to track who owes them money. That paper can't tell you your profit, can't warn you that you're running low on rice, and it's gone if the shop floods. Dedicated billing machines are too expensive and too complicated for these owners. This app does all of that on a phone they already own.

**Who uses it:**
The shop owner, who sets everything up and reads the reports at the end of the day. Some owners are not comfortable with technology or with English technical terms, so everything is designed to be tapped, not typed.
Behind the counter there can also be a manager, senior staff, and a cashier. Each person signs in with their own PIN, and each one only sees what the owner has allowed them to see. For example, a cashier can make sales but cannot see profit figures or change prices.

**What happens the first time:**
The owner opens the app and answers a few plain questions: the shop's name, their own name, their country and phone number (it defaults to Sri Lanka and checks the number is the right length). Then they pick what kind of shop they run — grocery, cafe, pharmacy, clothing, phones, stationery, salon, repair, flower shop, shoe shop, hardware, toys and gifts, or sports. The app then loads a starter list of 50 items that suit that kind of shop, so they don't face an empty screen. Then they say whether they want to track stock, whether they give credit to customers, and whether they want a cash drawer. They can add staff, connect a printer, and they're done.

**What you can do in it day to day:**

*Selling* — Tap items to build a bill. If an item comes in options (like a rice dish that's Regular or Full, or trousers that are Green in sizes 32/34/36/40 and Black in L/XL), a small window asks you to pick, and shows the price and how many are left for each choice. Anything out of stock goes grey but stays visible, so you can see it exists. You can set quantities, give a discount, attach a customer, add a note to the bill, and park a bill to come back to later. Payment can be cash (it works out the change), card, credit, a split between two methods, or QR.

*Bills* — Every bill ever made is kept and can be searched by number, customer, or who sold it. Any bill can be reprinted, refunded, or cancelled.

*Items* — The product list. You can add an item two ways: an easy step-by-step wizard that asks one question at a time, or a normal form with everything on one screen. Both save the same thing and you can switch between them without losing what you typed. Items can be edited, marked as favourites, or removed. Because the starter list is only a guess, you can tick several items at once and remove them together after one confirmation — useful for clearing out things your shop doesn't actually sell.

*Stock* — You can record a delivery when new stock arrives, or recount the shelf and correct the numbers. The app remembers why the number changed, not just what it is now.

*Customers and credit* — This replaces the notebook behind the counter. Each customer has a running balance and a limit, with a full record of what they took and what they paid back. The app warns you when someone is getting close to their limit.

*Suppliers and purchases* — Who you buy from, what deliveries you received, and what you still owe them.

*Expenses* — Rent, electricity, transport and so on, so the profit figure is honest.

*Cash drawer* — Optional. Open the day with a starting amount, record money going in and out, close the day by counting the drawer. It tells you if you're short.

*Reports* — What you sold, what it cost, what you earned, split by cash, card and credit, plus a trend chart.

*Things needing attention* — One screen that gathers low stock, money owed to you, and bills you need to pay, sorted by how urgent they are.

*Printing* — Connects to a cheap thermal receipt printer over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi. You choose the paper width, print a test page, and can set it to print automatically the moment a bill is paid. The receipt itself is customisable: shop name, a tagline or tax number, address, phone, date, cashier name, item count, a QR code, and a thank-you line.

*Backup* — The owner or manager can connect a Google account and the app will copy the shop's data to that account's Drive, automatically every hour or day, or on demand. Selling works perfectly with nothing connected; the backup is only a safety net.

*Alerts* — Thirteen kinds of event can raise a notification, and you choose which ones you actually want to hear about. A busy grocery doesn't want a buzz for every single sale.

*Activity log* — A record of who did what and when: refunds, price changes, sign-ins, and anything someone was blocked from doing.

*Staff* — Add your team, give each a role (owner, manager, senior staff, cashier), and then tick extra permissions on or off for each person individually.

**The rules the app is built around:**
Tap, don't type. Never ask anyone to type symbols like / or :. Never use computer jargon on screen — no "SKU", no "CSV". Grey out things that aren't available rather than hiding them. Never throw away something someone already entered. Big obvious buttons. Confirmations appear where people are looking. Nothing is pre-filled with fake demo customers or sales.

**Money and language:**
Sri Lankan Rupees, shown as Rs. 1,250.00. The interface is in English, and it defaults to Sri Lanka as the country.

**What's still not finished:**
- The app has never actually been compiled or run on a phone yet, so nothing has been tested in real use.
- Backups can be uploaded but there's no way to restore onto a new phone yet.
- The backup copy is not encrypted.
- Printing, the staff PIN screen, and the backup feature have all been written but never tried on a real device.
- It only supports one phone. Two phones are two separate shops as far as the app is concerned.
- Three of the four colour themes I designed are still just pictures, not actually built.
